# Blockout

**Up:** [[Vision Brain]] > [[3D & Blender Knowledge/00 - 3D & Blender Knowledge|3D & Blender Knowledge]] > [[3D & Blender Knowledge/03 - Modelling/00 - Modelling|Modelling]]

## What it is

Building the whole subject in primitives at correct real-world dimensions before adding any
detail. It is the cheapest stage and the one most often skipped.

## Why it matters

Proportion errors are invisible while you work and glaring when you finish. Fixing them after
detailing means redoing the detail. Blockout is where proportion is cheap.

It also answers questions that determine the rest of the job: how many pieces is this, what
repeats, where does the camera go, what is actually visible.

## Workflow

1. Set scene units and place a **human reference** if the subject has human scale.
2. Build the largest forms first, as primitives. Cubes and cylinders only.
3. Get the **silhouette** right - the shape read as a solid black shape against the background.
   If it does not read at this stage it will not read finished.
4. Check from **the intended camera angle**, not from a convenient orbit.
5. Only then decide the technical plan - see
   [[3D & Blender Knowledge/01 - Modelling Method|Modelling Method]].

## Judging a blockout

- Does the silhouette read?
- Are the proportions right against the reference?
- Are the dimensions real?
- Is it clear which parts repeat and which are unique?
- Is it clear what the camera will actually see?

If any answer is no, fix it now. Every one of these gets ten times more expensive later.

## When to skip it

Almost never, but honestly: for a single simple primitive-shaped object, or when the geometry is
fully determined by dimensions (a bracket from a drawing), blockout collapses into just modelling.

## Common mistakes

- Starting with detail on the most interesting part
- Blocking out without a scale reference
- Judging from a three-quarter orbit view when the shot is a straight-on elevation
- Refusing to change a blockout because time was already spent on it
