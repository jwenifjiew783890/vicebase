# Studio & Product Lighting

**Up:** [[Vision Brain]] > [[3D & Blender Knowledge/00 - 3D & Blender Knowledge|3D & Blender Knowledge]] > [[3D & Blender Knowledge/08 - Lighting/00 - Lighting|Lighting]]

## What it is

Controlled lighting for a subject presented on its own - products, hero assets, turntables.

## The principle

In studio work you are lighting **the material**, not the space. The job is to show form, edge and
surface quality clearly. Reflective and matte subjects need almost opposite treatment.

## Setup that works

1. **Neutral backdrop** - a curved sweep avoids a horizon line behind the subject
2. **Large key**, usually a big area light or softbox at 30-45 degrees, high
3. **Fill** - a large, weak source or a bounce card opposite
4. **Rim** from behind to separate subject from background
5. **Bounce cards** as geometry, exactly as in a real studio - often better than adding more lights

## Lighting reflective subjects

A glossy or metal object shows **reflections of its surroundings**, not the lights themselves.
Lighting it means arranging what it reflects.

- Use **large soft sources** - they produce broad, readable highlights that describe the form
- Small lights give tiny bright dots that describe nothing
- Add geometry to reflect - cards, panels - not just lights
- A metal in an empty scene renders dark. That is correct.

## Lighting matte subjects

Matte surfaces show light direction and shadow rather than reflections, so key direction and
shadow quality do the work. A harder key reads better than it would on chrome.

## Showing edges

Edge highlights are what make hard-surface objects read as manufactured. They require:

- bevels on the geometry - without them there is no edge to catch light
- a light positioned so its reflection grazes the edges
- often a dedicated rim light for exactly this

## Common mistakes

- Small lights on reflective subjects, giving dot highlights and no form
- Backdrop with a visible seam or horizon
- Over-lighting until all shadow is gone, so the subject flattens
- Forgetting that bounce cards are geometry, and adding a fourth light instead
- Lighting a product with no bevels and blaming the material for looking synthetic
