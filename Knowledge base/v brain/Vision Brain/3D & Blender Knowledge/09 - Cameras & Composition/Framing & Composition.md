# Framing & Composition

**Up:** [[Vision Brain]] > [[3D & Blender Knowledge/00 - 3D & Blender Knowledge|3D & Blender Knowledge]] > [[3D & Blender Knowledge/09 - Cameras & Composition/00 - Cameras & Composition|Cameras & Composition]]

## What it is

Arranging what is in frame so the image reads clearly and directs attention.

## Structure first

Before refinement, get the structure right:

- **What is the subject?** If the answer is unclear, the composition is unclear.
- **Where does the eye go first, and where next?** Lines, contrast and focus create that path.
- **Does the silhouette read?** Squint at it, or view as a black-and-white thumbnail. If it reads
  as a shape, the composition works.

## The useful devices

| Device | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Rule of thirds | Off-centre placement, generally more dynamic than centring |
| Leading lines | Edges and paths that carry the eye to the subject |
| Framing elements | Foreground shapes that enclose the subject |
| Depth layering | Foreground, midground, background - the main tool for depth |
| Negative space | Empty area giving the subject room to read |
| Contrast | Tonal, colour or focus difference isolating the subject |

**Symmetry and centring are legitimate choices**, not failures - for formal architecture, they are
often the correct one.

## Depth

A flat-looking render usually lacks layering. Depth comes from:

- something in the foreground, even out of focus
- overlapping forms, so occlusion signals order
- atmospheric perspective - distant objects lower in contrast and shifted toward the ambient colour
- lighting that separates planes

**Overlap is the strongest depth cue available** and costs nothing.

## Camera height

Height carries meaning and is often left at whatever it defaulted to:

- **Eye level** (~1.6 m) - neutral, natural, the correct default for architecture
- **Low** - subject appears larger, more imposing
- **High** - subject appears smaller, gives an overview
- **Ground level** - dramatic, and rarely how a space is experienced

For architectural visualisation, eye level is almost always right, because it shows the space as it
will actually be experienced.

## Checking

- View through the camera with passepartout, at final aspect ratio - not in a perspective orbit
- Check as a small thumbnail; composition problems are more obvious small
- Check in greyscale to judge tonal structure without colour

## Common mistakes

- Composing in the viewport instead of through the camera
- No foreground, so the image is flat
- Subject centred by accident rather than choice
- Tangents - edges that just touch, creating visual confusion
- Final aspect ratio not set, so framing changes at render time
