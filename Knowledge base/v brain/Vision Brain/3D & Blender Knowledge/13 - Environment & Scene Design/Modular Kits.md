# Modular Kits

**Up:** [[Vision Brain]] > [[3D & Blender Knowledge/00 - 3D & Blender Knowledge|3D & Blender Knowledge]] > [[3D & Blender Knowledge/13 - Environment & Scene Design/00 - Environment & Scene Design|Environment & Scene Design]]

## What it is

A set of pieces designed to combine - wall sections, floors, corners, doorways, trim - from which
many structures can be assembled.

## Why it is the right approach for buildings

A modular kit turns "model this building" into "model twelve pieces and assemble". The second is
faster, more consistent, easier to change and far lighter, because every piece is instanced.

## The grid is the whole discipline

Pick a grid unit and hold to it absolutely. Common choices are 1 m, 2 m or 4 m for architecture.
Every piece must be an exact multiple.

- **Origins on the grid**, at a consistent corner
- **Dimensions exact multiples** - 2.0 m, never 1.998 m
- **Snapping to increments** while assembling
- **Apply scale** on every piece, or dimensions lie

**One piece slightly off-grid produces gaps that accumulate** across an assembly, and finding it
afterwards is miserable.

## Designing the set

Start with the minimum viable kit:

- Straight wall
- Corner, inner and outer
- Doorway and window variants
- Floor and ceiling
- Trim, edges and caps to hide joins

Then add variation: damaged variants, alternative materials, decorative pieces.

**Trim pieces hide joins**, which is what makes a modular assembly stop reading as modular.

## Avoiding the modular look

The weakness is visible repetition. Counter it with:

- several variants of the most-used pieces
- rotation and mirroring where it does not break the silhouette
- unique set-dressing over the modular base
- material variation across pieces
- deliberately breaking the grid with non-modular hero elements

## Common mistakes

- Non-exact dimensions, giving cumulative gaps
- Inconsistent origin placement, so pieces do not snap
- Too few variants, so repetition is obvious
- No trim, leaving visible seams at every join
- Unapplied scale on kit pieces
