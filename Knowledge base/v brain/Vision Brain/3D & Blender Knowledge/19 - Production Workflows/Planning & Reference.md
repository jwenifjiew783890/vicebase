# Planning & Reference

**Up:** [[Vision Brain]] > [[3D & Blender Knowledge/00 - 3D & Blender Knowledge|3D & Blender Knowledge]] > [[3D & Blender Knowledge/19 - Production Workflows/00 - Production Workflows|Production Workflows]]

## What it is

The work done before geometry exists. It has the highest return of any stage and is the one most
often skipped.

## Establish the requirement

Before anything: **what is this for, and how will it be judged?**

- Final output - still, animation, real-time asset, print, fabrication?
- Resolution and how large it will be seen
- Camera angles, or free rotation?
- Deadline, and therefore what quality is achievable
- Who receives it, and in what format

Every one of these changes the technical approach. Discovering them late means rework.

## Reference

**Never model from memory.** Gather:

- **Orthographic views** for anything man-made - front, side, top
- **Dimensioned drawings** where accuracy matters
- **Detail photographs** of materials, wear and junctions
- **Context photographs** showing the subject in its setting
- **Lighting reference** - a photograph of the intended mood is worth more than describing it

Organise reference so it is visible while working, not buried in a folder.

## The technical plan

For anything non-trivial, decide before starting:

- modelling approach per component
- what is modular or instanced
- polygon and texture budget, if there is one
- what is modelled versus textured
- what stays procedural
- naming and file structure

Writing this down takes ten minutes and prevents the most expensive category of rework - the
kind discovered halfway through.

## Estimating

Experienced practitioners estimate by breaking work into parts they have done before. The
recurring underestimates:

- **set dressing and detail** - almost always longer than the hero modelling
- **materials and texturing**
- **lighting iteration**
- **fixing problems found at render time**
- **revisions after review**

A useful heuristic: modelling is often less than half the total.

## Common mistakes

- Starting geometry before the requirement is clear
- Reference gathered after modelling begins
- No dimensional reference on something that must be accurate
- No plan, so decisions are made ad hoc and inconsistently
- Estimating only the modelling
