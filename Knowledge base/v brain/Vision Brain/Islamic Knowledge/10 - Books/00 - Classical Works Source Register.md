## Why nothing was imported — the licence problem is specific, not vague

A common assumption is that a classical work is automatically free to copy because its author
died centuries ago. **That is not sufficient.** What circulates today is almost always a
*modern critical edition*: a named editor has established the text, added vocalisation,
footnotes, volume and page divisions, and an apparatus. That editorial work attracts its own
copyright even where the author's words do not.

Concretely, for `Majmu' al-Fatawa`: the 37-volume compilation was **collected and arranged by
Abd al-Rahman ibn Muhammad ibn Qasim** in the twentieth century. Copies on Archive.org carry
**no declared licence**. So the honest status is `UNKNOWN`, and
[[Islamic Knowledge/99 - Source & Authenticity Rules|Source Rules §5]] plus your own standing
instruction — *do not claim something is public domain without verification* — mean it stays
out of the corpus until a specific edition's licence is actually established.

## OpenITI — a source that DOES permit reproduction

Verified 2026-09-03: the **Open Islamicate Texts Initiative** corpus
(<https://openiti.org>, releases at <https://github.com/OpenITI/RELEASE>) is licensed
**CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** — redistribution and reuse permitted with attribution, non-commercial,
share-alike. It holds **125 works of Ibn Taymiyyah** and **45 of Ibn al-Qayyim** as
machine-readable Arabic text.

This is the correct route for the classical scholars, and supersedes the "no licensed source
found" position previously recorded here. One work has been imported as a verified sample
(*al-Aqidah al-Wasitiyyah*); the bulk import is pending only because GitHub's unauthenticated
API rate limit (60 requests/hour) blocks enumerating the remaining file paths.

**Edition caveat:** OpenITI versions derive from specific digital editions and do not preserve
printed page numbering. Cite the source URL, never a page number.

### Al-Albani (d. 1420 AH)

His **gradings** are already in the corpus at scale — **19,081** of them, attributed by name,
across six collections. See
[[Islamic Knowledge/09 - Scholars/03 - Al-Albani/Al-Albani|his scholar note]].

His **books** (*Silsilat al-Ahadith al-Sahihah*, *al-Da'ifah*, *Irwa' al-Ghalil*, *Sahih
al-Jami'*) are **twentieth-century works under active copyright** — not imported, and no
licence permitting reproduction was found.

### Salih al-Fawzan (b. 1354 AH)

Contemporary works, under copyright. His official site `alfawzan.af.org.sa` was **unreachable**
when checked (2026-09-03), consistent with reporting that he withdrew his online material in
April 2026. **No mirrors or archives were used** — if the author withdrew the material
deliberately, retrieving it from elsewhere is your decision to make, not this system's.

## What *is* held in full, and why

The contrast is instructive and worth stating plainly:

| Source | Licence position | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Qur'an (Tanzil / King Fahd Complex) | CC BY 3.0 | **Full text imported** |
| Hadith (`fawazahmed0/hadith-api`) | Unlicense / public domain | **Full text imported** |
| **binbaz.org.sa** | *"النقل متاح لكل مسلم بشرط ذكر المصدر"* — transmission permitted with attribution | **Full text imported** |
| binothaimeen.net | All rights reserved © | Link only |
| **OpenITI corpus** | **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** | **Full text permitted** — 1 work imported, 169 available |
| Other classical editions | `UNKNOWN` | Pointer only |

The corpus is large where permission is explicit and thin where it is not. That asymmetry is
the licence position showing through honestly, not a gap in effort.

## To add a work here

Establish the **specific edition's** licence — publisher, editor, year, and an actual licence
statement. Record it, then import only what that licence permits.
